# The next desk should have an answer

Research and scope update: 12 October 2026.

## Passenger account

A traveller left a laptop bag on an ICE journey when disembarking in Nürnberg. According to the passenger account, railway loss and police reports were submitted promptly. At a subsequent station visit, staff quoted a minimum three-day wait and said they could not contact other stations or provide that level of visibility.

This account motivates the design. It does not establish a universal DB waiting rule, lack of internal communication, or the precise root cause. Exact service identity, journey endpoint, report timestamps and redacted incident evidence still need confirmation. Employer/client names and actual report identifiers are excluded.

## Desired experience

The owner raises one request. Relevant remaining-route, onboard and terminal teams receive accepted tasks and report back. A desk visited the next morning opens the shared case and explains what was checked, what is pending and whether an item was recorded elsewhere. A Hamburg find should be visible to a Nürnberg desk; collection travel is advised only after ownership and readiness are confirmed.

## Existing system — avoid duplication

DB publicly documents online reporting, matching, successful-find notification and a link to edit/search/cancel a report. Its supplier RUBICON describes NovaFind deployment since 2019, continued matching with later found items and digital networking of DB lost-property offices. Public NovaFind manuals cover history, workflow statuses, roles, mobile preregistration and storage transfers.

DB has 3-S station coordination infrastructure. Secondary and historical sources indicate staff-assisted enquiries while trains travel, but current reviewed primary guidance does not guarantee that an online loss report automatically dispatches an onboard check.

Public evidence does not establish the exact early-search assignment/acceptance policy, response escalation, or passenger-visible physical-check timeline. Absence of published detail is not proof the internal functionality is absent.

## Hypothesis and proposed controls

| Situation | Proposed response | Needed validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No visibility at the desk | Shared team responses across participating desks | Current DB access and workflow |
| Report received, check unknown | Separate created, accepted and completed task states | Actual staff acceptance process |
| One station finds nothing | Preserve scope/time and remaining tasks | Appropriate check definitions |
| Bag registered elsewhere | Link location and inventory reference | Existing-system integration |
| Team cannot check | Record reason; keep report open; coordinate follow-up | Staffing and routing ownership |
| Urgent essentials | Proposed triage and escalation targets | Capacity, urgency policy and alternatives |

## Demonstration boundary

All routes, recipients, items, timestamps of actions and ownership checks in the app are synthetic. No railway staff are messaged. Fixture routes illustrate recipients; they are not actual ICE schedules. The demo has unrestricted shared synthetic access and cannot process real passenger records safely.

## Evaluation

Validate the actual process with station, onboard, terminal and lost-property staff. Compare acknowledgment and response times, unanswered tasks, desk visibility, check-scope completeness, staff workload, passenger comprehension and duplicate/false claims. Do not claim recovery-rate or financial improvements without a controlled pilot baseline.
